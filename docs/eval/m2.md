# M2 affect and expression: exit report

Measured on 2026-10-17 (SPEC [section 14](../specs/kickoff.md), Milestones, M2 exit: "robot reacts
distinctly to known adult, known child and stranger with no LLM, and moves through awake, drowsy
and resting on a replayed 24-hour light log"). Everything here ran on one Mac (Apple M3 Pro, 12
logical cores, Darwin 25.6.0, Python 3.12.13) against the MuJoCo Reachy Mini daemon
(`reachy_mini` 1.12.0 as installed in the test environment, headless; the robot runs 1.11, and the driver notes say both were checked). **Nothing was measured on the real robot**: antenna and pitch
signs, light calibration, reflex latency through the bridge and the SDK, real audio and every
threshold are open (see "Sim-only and unverified on hardware").

Code version: `m2/exit` at the commit that adds this file (the exit tests are in the same
commits). The affect state in all of this is an expressive model that drives gestures, not a claim
that the robot has feelings (SPEC [section 12](../specs/kickoff.md), Behaviour and content).

## Summary

| Exit check | Result | Where it runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Known adult, known child and stranger each get their own affect label, register and expression amplitude, with no LLM | pass | `pytest -m sim` (`test_sim.py`), and the same probe on a mocked SDK client in plain `pytest` (`test_app_exit.py`) |
| The child and the stranger are never approached (body yaw stays 0 while the same expression moves the adult's body); minor caps are on for both | pass | same |
| The stranger's reaction is low intensity | pass: its expression factor is 0.236 against 0.677 (adult) and 1.208 (child); the same gesture is 2.6 degrees against 10.7 and 10.6 | same |
| Awake, drowsy, resting, drowsy, awake on a replayed 24 hour light log, rest posture held, idle motion stopped | pass: 23.5 s of wall time at 3600x through the real app, runner and motion loop | `test_app_replay.py` (plain `pytest`); the model alone in `test_circadian_runner.py` (9 ms) |

The sim test is slow for a unit test (about 62 s for the three runs, 44 s before the provoked phase
waited for quiet) because each run waits for the fail-safe minor caps to release (5 s of empty face
frames) before the person walks in.

## A. Adult, child, stranger (SPEC 5, 7, 12, 14)

**Protocol.** The whole app runs (`RoboEmotApp.run`: pump thread, tracker, matcher, registry,
appraisal, affect engine, register tracker, expression renderer, attention, motion loop); only the
face detector, the face embedder and the camera are stand-ins, as in the M1 exit test (`Scene` in
`test_sim.py`). The same planted face, 25 degrees to the robot's left, is one of:

* **adult**: enrolled with declared age group `adult`;
* **child**: enrolled with declared age group `child` (consent with a guardian);
* **stranger**: nobody enrolled. There is no attribute model, so the face's age is `unknown`, which
  counts as a possible minor (SPEC 5).

Each run starts a fresh app and registry. The probe (`exit_support.py`) waits until nobody is in view
and the minor caps have released (face frames with no face for `safety.minor_hold_s`, 5 s) and no
expression is playing, then the face appears. It waits on published state, never on a fixed sleep:
`affect.state` for the label and a new labelled impulse, a counter on the expression renderer for
each expression started, `register.active` for the register, and the robot's own state for the
motion (sim: the simulated joints, polled every 10 ms; app level: the targets the motion loop wrote to the
driver). Two phases per person:

* **arrival**: what the shipped rules do by themselves when the person appears.
* **provoked**: the same labelled curiosity impulse (valence 0.3, arousal 0.3) fed to the affect
  engine after the label hold and any follow-on expression have ended (`_quiet`, so the next
  expression is the one the impulse causes). The impulse is the same for everyone but lands on top
  of each person's own state; the register's share is separated out in the assertions.

The shipped Reachy Mini expression map has no `body_yaw` channel, so "never approached" would hold
trivially. The test therefore adds a 12 degree `body_yaw` channel to `calm`, `joy` and `curiosity`
(app config, `BODY_MAP_YAML`); the renderer's approach gate (`register.approach_allowed`, SPEC 7,
Safety) is then the only thing keeping the child's and the stranger's body still.

**Measured (simulated robot, one run; the test passed on every run after the last change to the
probe).** Angles are degrees away from the manifest home. "Factor" is the renderer's amplitude
factor before the minor cap (`register.motion_amplitude x intensity_factor[reaction] x (0.5 + 0.5 x
affect intensity) x circadian factor`). "Caps" is the motion loop's minor flag when the expression
started. The provoked impulse lands after the label hold and the follow-on expression have ended,
so the state it adds to has decayed from the arrival.

| Person | Phase | Affect label | Valence / arousal | Affect intensity | Register (amp, reaction, approach) | Caps | Expression, factor | Antenna peak | Head yaw peak | Body yaw peak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| adult | arrival | calm | +0.50 / +0.24 | 0.35 | adult (1.0, medium, yes) | on | calm, 0.677 | 1.54 | 19.7 | 0.00 |
| adult | provoked | curiosity | +0.51 / +0.51 | 0.42 | adult (1.0, medium, yes) | off | curiosity, 0.711 | 10.67 | 28.6 | 8.48 |
| child | arrival | joy | +0.70 / +0.54 | 0.55 | child (1.2, high, no) | on | joy, 1.208 | 3.51 | 14.7 | 0.00 |
| child | provoked | curiosity | +0.57 / +0.63 | 0.51 | child (1.2, high, no) | on | curiosity, 1.176 | 10.58 | 15.2 | 0.00 |
| stranger | arrival | curiosity | +0.05 / +0.37 | 0.12 | unknown (0.7, low, no) | on | curiosity, 0.236 | 2.12 | 15.1 | 0.00 |

The settled robot's own antenna motion before the person arrived was 0.01 degrees in every run (the
probe waits for head, body and antennas to be within 3 degrees of home first).
The same probe on a mocked SDK client (commanded targets, no physics) gives the same affect,
register and factor columns to within 0.01; its antenna peaks are 1.60 / 10.67 (adult), 3.32 / 10.58
(child) and 2.13 / 2.57 (stranger) degrees, its adult body yaw 8.54, and its head yaw 5.78 degrees
for all three: the bearing of the fake detector's face, with no overshoot.

What the table shows, and the assertions that follow from it (`check_exit` in `exit_support.py`):

* **Affect differs.** Labels calm, joy and curiosity; valence is positive for all three but ordered
  child > adult > stranger (0.70, 0.50, 0.05) and so is the affect intensity (0.55, 0.35, 0.12). The
  valence sign alone does not tell adult from stranger; the label does (the test asserts the
  ordering, not a sign).
* **Register differs.** `adult`, `child`, `unknown` with the schema defaults (amplitude 1.0 / 1.2 /
  0.7, reaction medium / high / low, approach yes / no / no). Group names come from `register.active`.
* **Expression amplitude differs per register.** Arrival factors 1.208 > 0.677 > 0.236 and provoked
  factors 1.176 > 0.711 > 0.286 (child, adult, stranger). The provoked impulse is the same for all
  three but adds to each person's own state, so the affect term differs a little; dividing it out
  (`floor + (1 - floor) x intensity`, floor 0.5) leaves the register term alone: 1.56 (child,
  1.2 x 1.3), 1.00 (adult) and 0.42 (stranger, 0.7 x 0.6), and the test asserts that ordering.
  Measured on the provoked curiosity (15 degree antenna keyframe): 10.67 degrees for the adult
  (15 x 0.711), 10.58 for the child (15 x 1.176 = 17.6, cut to 60% by the minor cap) and 2.57 for
  the stranger (15 x 0.286 = 4.3, cut to 60%). The child's measured peak is therefore about the
  adult's, because the cap and not the register limits it; the test asserts the stranger at under
  half of the others for the measured size.
* **Head motion.** All three are looked at (attention is not gated by the register). The adult's
  head follows the planted bearing (25 degrees) and overshoots to 28.6 degrees in the simulator;
  the mocked-client twin, which reads the limiter's commands, peaks at the bearing exactly (5.78
  degrees), so the excess is the simulated servos' dynamics and not the attention or the limiter.
  The sim test bounds the adult at 25 + 5 degrees and the twin at the bearing + 1. The child's and
  the stranger's head reaches 14.7 to 15.2 degrees, which is `safety.minor_motion_factor` (0.6) times
  25; the test asserts that range.
* **Approach.** Body yaw peak is 8.48 degrees for the adult (the 12 degree channel times 0.711) once
  the caps have released, and under 0.5 degrees, in fact 0.00, for the child and the stranger in both
  phases, with `approach_allowed` false in the register.
* **Stranger is low intensity** (above): reaction `low`, smallest affect intensity, factor 0.236, and
  a gesture about a quarter the size of the others for the same impulse.

### Findings

* **The first reaction to a recognised adult is under the minor caps.** Until the face is
  recognised it is a `percept.face` of unknown age, so it is a possible minor, and the caps (and
  the register's `approach_allowed false`, which `resolve_register` forces while the flag is on) stay
  on for `safety.minor_hold_s` (5 s) after the last such face. The adult's `calm` therefore played
  at 0.6 of its range with no body channel (1.54 degrees of antenna), and the adult is only approached
  from the next reaction on, a few seconds later. This is the fail-safe of SPEC 5 and 12 working, not
  a bug. The test asserts it (`adult.arrival.minor_caps`) as a documented limitation, not a
  requirement: if recognition ever comes before the first face percept, that line is the one to
  relax. It also means "distinct" for the adult is
  shown cleanly by the provoked phase, not the arrival.
* **`calm` is followed by `curiosity` for the adult.** The `calm` label is held for
  `affect.event_label_hold_s` (3 s); after that the nearest label prototype to valence 0.5, arousal
  0.24 is `curiosity`, and a second expression starts (the 15 degree antenna keyframe at 0.676).
  Measured per expression (the probe cuts each span at the next start), so the 1.54 degrees above is
  the calm gesture only. Whether a recognised adult should end up looking curious is a tuning
  question about the prototypes, not something this test decides.
* **Valence alone is a weak discriminator.** `person.unknown_appears` is +0.05 valence by design
  (SPEC 7), so adult against stranger rests on the label and on arousal, and on the register.
* **No wiring change was needed** to make the three cases distinct; the only code added for the
  tests is a public counter, `ExpressionRenderer.started`, so tests wait on a start instead of
  comparing the last label and amplitude.
* **The joy flap is mostly clipped in the simulator.** The child's `joy` (18 degree antenna
  keyframe, 0.45 s cycle, repeated 3 times) at factor 1.208 and the 0.6 cap should reach about 13
  degrees; the simulated antennas peak at 3.5 to 4.4 degrees across runs. The motion limiter's
  conservative fallback velocity and acceleration (the manifest dynamics are unmeasured) clip the
  fast flap; the slower `curiosity` (0.5 s rise) reaches its commanded size. This is why the
  measured size is compared on the provoked `curiosity` and not on the arrival gestures.

## B. Replayed 24 hour light log (SPEC 7A, 14)

**Protocol.** `roboemot.circadian.replay.synthetic_day()`: one sample per minute from 08:00, dawn
06:30 to 07:30, daylight, an evening lamp from 18:30, lights off at 22:15, with noise and a fixed
seed (7); 1,440 samples.

* **Model alone** (`test_circadian_runner.py`, `replay`): the samples go through the sampler and
  estimator (hysteresis, 60 s confirmation) and the runner ticks once per sample on a fake clock.
  Wall time 9 ms. Transitions: drowsy 21:14 (21.23 h), resting 22:30, drowsy again 07:00, awake
  07:01; `light.off` at 22:16 and `light.on` at 06:49 as events. Already covered by the existing
  tests; not repeated.
* **Through the app** (`test_app_replay.py`, new): the real `RoboEmotApp.run` with the real
  circadian runner, affect runner, expression renderer and motion loop, on a mocked SDK client. The
  clock is virtual, 3600 times real time, started when the runner's first update has run (the
  start-up takes seconds of real time and would otherwise eat virtual hours). One logged sample per
  virtual minute goes straight into the runner's sampler from a feeder thread, as the model replay
  does: the camera path cannot deliver a frame per virtual minute at this speed (it read a frame
  every few virtual hours, and the light went stale). A camera source with the same brightness runs
  as well. Nobody is in view.

| Check | Measured |
| --- | --- |
| State sequence | awake, drowsy, resting, drowsy, awake |
| Hours of entry (virtual, one run) | awake 08:00, drowsy 21:17 (21.28 h), resting 22:33 (22.55 h), drowsy 07:00 (7.01 h), awake 07:04 (7.06 h) |
| Wall time | 23.4 s for the 23 virtual hours from 08:00 to 07:05 the next morning (the feeder holds the full 24 hours of samples, the test stops once the robot is awake again); the test fails above 60 s |
| While resting | rest posture command fed at least 50 times in the span, no `idle` or `idle-additive` command in it; head pitch above half the rest pose (20 degrees) times the unknown register's amplitude (0.7), right antenna below 0 and left above 0 (droop outward), all three at once, asserted on the interior of the span (20 feed-log entries trimmed at each edge) |
| On waking | a `stretch` primitive is fed, then the commanded head pitch returns below 0.1 rad |

The test compares the hours to the offline replay of the same log (21.23, 22.50, 7.00 and 7.02 h)
with a tolerance of half an hour of virtual time, so the bounds do not depend on how steady the
machine's clock is; the measured hours above are within 0.06 h of it. They are results, not
numbers the SPEC states. The thresholds in the run are the defaults: drowsy above 0.55
sleepiness plus 0.05 hysteresis, resting above 0.75 or in the quiet hours (22:30 to 07:00), model
update every 10 s.

## Fixes made in this slice

* **Reflex: a close face that arrives during a watchdog hold keeps its arm** (SPEC 10, 12).
  `ReflexEngine.on_tick` collected the triggers (and disarmed the proximity rule) before the
  `core.holding` return, so the trigger was dropped and the face, still close, did not withdraw
  until it had left and come back. The dropped triggers now re-arm their rule, and so does a close
  face dropped as stale. Regression test:
  `test_a_close_face_arriving_during_the_watchdog_hold_still_withdraws_once_the_hold_ends`
  (fails without the fix). Disabled reflexes drop the edge the same way, so enabling them with a
  face still close withdraws once; a stale or future-stamped trigger re-arms too; a cooldown or a
  minor suppression still consumes the arm until the face leaves: the face is meant to stay
  unanswered then. Each case has its own test in `test_reflex.py`, and SPEC section 10 says so.
* `ExpressionRenderer.started` (counter), above.

## Sim-only and unverified on hardware

Everything above is the simulator or a mocked client. Open, and listed in SPEC section 15:

* **Antenna signs.** Droop outward and perk up are inferred from the home pose; the sim moves the
  joints the way the manifest says, which cannot show that the manifest has the right sign for the
  real antennas. Expression maps and the rest posture (`rest_pose_deg`: head pitch 20, antennas
  -50 / +50 degrees) are starting points never seen on the robot.
* **Pitch sign and "never forward".** A negative `head.pitch` is taken as nose up; `withdraw` and
  `startle` depend on it. No sim test of the reflexes was added (head pose read-back timing makes it
  flaky).
* **Light calibration.** The sim has no camera: the light comes from a log or from a stand-in
  frame. `light.calibration_k` is unset, exposure and gain through the SDK are unchecked, and
  `dark_luma` 0.12, `bright_luma` 0.35, the noise rule and the hysteresis are untested on the IMX708.
* **Reflex latency through the bridge and the SDK audio chunking.** The reflex budget (100 ms
  audio, 250 ms vision on the CM4) is asserted on a fake clock and, through real threads, against a
  scripted microphone; the real chunk size and delay of `get_audio_sample`, and the bridge hop, are
  not measured.
* **Real audio and direction of arrival.** The loud-onset floor and ratio, the head-relative DoA and
  the half-plane ambiguity were never fed by a real microphone array.
* **Thresholds.** The appraisal values, the register defaults, the intensity factors, the label
  prototypes, the 5 s minor hold, the 0.4 "too close" face height and the sleepiness thresholds are
  judgement values. This report shows that they give three distinct reactions and a plausible day;
  it does not show that they feel right to people, or that a child's `joy` at 1.2 reads as friendly
  and not alarming.
* **Identity in the loop.** The face embedder and detector are stand-ins here; recognition quality
  is the M1 report's business ([m1.md](m1.md)). The age is declared for the adult and the child; an
  estimated child (no declared age) is not part of this test.
* **CM4.** No timing here says anything about the Raspberry Pi.

## Reproduce

```bash
python3.12 -m venv .venv && . .venv/bin/activate
pip install -e "packages/roboemot-core[dev]" -e "packages/roboemot-reachymini[dev,sim]"
export OMP_NUM_THREADS=2
nice -n 10 pytest packages/roboemot-reachymini/tests/test_app_exit.py packages/roboemot-reachymini/tests/test_app_replay.py -s
scripts/sim-daemon.sh &          # headless MuJoCo daemon on port 8000; stop it afterwards
nice -n 10 pytest -m sim -k distinctly -s packages/roboemot-reachymini/tests/test_sim.py
kill %1
```

`-s` prints one line per phase with the numbers of the tables. The app-level exit test takes about
40 s, the replay about 24 s.
