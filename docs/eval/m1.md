# M1 perception evaluation report

Measured on 2026-10-16 with the code of `epic/m1-perception` (SPEC [section 14](../specs/kickoff.md),
"Perception eval"; M1 exit: "eval report published"). Everything here was measured on one Mac
(Apple M3 Pro, 12 logical cores, Darwin 25.6.0), with ONNX Runtime 1.31.0 and OpenCV
5.0.0 on Python 3.13.13. **The Raspberry Pi CM4 was not
measured**: every latency below is a Mac number and says nothing about the 2.5 s CM4 budget yet.
The scripts are in `scripts/eval/`, the raw aggregates in `scripts/eval/results/`.

All runs go through the shipped code, not the spike's reimplementations: `YuNetDetector`,
`SFaceEmbedder`, `FairFaceEstimator`, `NanoDetDetector` and `DinoV2Embedder`
(`roboemot.vision.onnx`, loaded with `load_stages` / `load_object_detector` from the verified
model files), `VisionPipeline` with `LatestFrameRunner` and `pump`, `AttributeWorker`, the registry
`Matcher`, and `AutoEnroller` (`roboemot.world.autoenroll`), all with the default configuration
unless a row says otherwise.

## Identity (SPEC 6, 14)

**Protocol.** LFW, all 13,233 images of 5,749 people, open set, run through `YuNetDetector` (320x240
input) and `SFaceEmbedder`. Only faces the pipeline would embed count (detector score at least
`vision.tracker.high_score` 0.6 and a shorter box side of at least `vision.embed_min_face_px` 40);
all 13,233 images passed, and the face nearest the image centre is the subject
(2,279 images contain more than one detection). Each draw enrolls `k`
people who have at least 11 images (143 such people exist) with 5 photos each, through
`Registry.enroll`; every other photo of the whole dataset is a probe. Strangers are everybody not
enrolled. Threshold 0.45, margin 0.1, 3 of 5 votes, as shipped; online refresh is off so probes do
not change the gallery.

* **Per evaluation:** one photo against the registry, `Matcher` with 1 of 1 vote and the shipped
  threshold and margin. A false accept is a stranger photo accepted as anyone; a false reject is an
  enrolled photo not accepted as its owner (rejected, or named as someone else).
* **Per track:** 5 different photos of one person as consecutive frames through `VisionPipeline`
  (0.3 s apart, the first repeated once because the tracker reports a track after 2 hits), shipped
  3 of 5 voting. Enrolled people: 6 random 5-photo subsets of their non-enrolment photos. Strangers:
  300 random people per draw. LFW photos are independent shots, not video frames.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 enrolled (home scale) | 10 | 0.120% (0.102 to 0.140) | 0.290% (0.140 to 0.597) | 0/3,000 (0.00% (0.00 to 0.13)) | 0/600 (0.00% (0.00 to 0.64)) |

independent, so they are optimistic. Details:

  (12 of those had a best match above the threshold and lost on the
  0.1 margin) and 3 were someone else's name. The per-evaluation false accept
  rate varied between 0.46%
  and 1.88% across the
  3 draws (10 enrolled: 0.05%
  to 0.23%); I did not break it down by stranger.
* Tracks that the tracker split in two (a second track id in the last frame, so fewer votes on the
  main track): 18 of 600 (10 enrolled) and
  40 of 1,800 (100 enrolled). They still ended
  correct here.
* Reference from the spike, LFW pairs with the same models (`scripts/bench/results/lfw.json`):
  accuracy 0.9892, TAR
  0.968 at a pair FAR of 1e-4 (cosine
  threshold 0.419, from
  29,640,749 impostor pairs) and
  0.980 at 1e-3. The shipped threshold

figures. The enrolled people are the ones with the most photos, which are the easiest. Enrollment
here uses LFW photos, not the guided enrollment flow with its quality gates on a real camera.
Children, low light, glasses, masks, angles and a camera at a different height are not covered.
The false reject rates are therefore lower bounds.

## Age group and gender (SPEC 5, 14)

**Protocol.** FairFace validation set (10,954 faces, one image per face), a seeded random subset of
1400 faces (the spike's indices) for INT8 and its first
600 faces for FP32. INT8 is what the cm4 profile loads
(`load_stages(profile="cm4")`), FP32 is the default elsewhere. Each face becomes a 5-frame sequence
through `VisionPipeline`: YuNet detection, tracker, the product crop (box plus 25% margin), 3 crops
at least 0.3 s apart, one `AttributeWorker` job, `percept.face`. **FairFace has one photo per
face, so the 3 crops are simulated**: the original and two random variants (shift up to 6%, scale
0.95 to 1.06, rotation up to 4 degrees, brightness 0.9 to 1.1). This stands in for head motion
between frames and is not real video. Aggregation and the 0.8 confidence rule are the shipped
`estimate_age_group` and `estimate_gender`. YuNet found a face in all 1,400 images, so no face
was lost before the estimator. FairFace gives age in bins; 10-19 cannot be split at 13 and is scored
as `teen`; "minor" in the table means under 20, "child" means under 10. Gender uses FairFace's
labels (male, female) and nothing else, which is a limitation of the data.

"Error of known" counts only faces where the estimate is not `unknown`. An unknown age counts as a
possible minor in the product (SPEC 5), so unknown is the safe outcome and a wrong adult label for
a minor is the unsafe one.

### Findings

* **The age estimate separates child from adult and nothing else.** In the confusion table below no
  teenager and no senior was ever labelled teen or senior. Most teenagers (66.5%) and almost all seniors
  (92.6%) come out `unknown`, which behaves as "possible minor" (safe), but 12.9%
  of minors under 20 (46/357) come out adult. By group:
  teens 28.4%, children under 10 1.0%.
  Part of the teen rate is the 18-19 year olds in the 10-19 bin, who are legally adults.
  16.6% of adults and seniors are treated as possible minors
  (173/1,043), which is the cost of the safe direction.
* Single-crop estimates (first crop alone, 0.8 rule) were within noise of the 3-crop aggregate:
  minors labelled adult 12.6% against
  12.9%, gender error 3.7% against
  4.4%. On this data the extra crops do not buy accuracy, partly because the
  simulated crops are near copies of the first.
* Gender error and gender unknown rates are highest for Black women (12.0% error, 15.3% unknown, n=98) and Indian women
  (9.1% error). The Black group also has the most unknown ages
  (37.6%). Cells hold about 50 to 130 faces, so differences of a few points are noise.
* INT8 against FP32 on the same 600 faces: minors labelled adult 15.3% (23/150) against
  14.0% (21/150), age unknown 25.3% against 26.3%,
  gender error of known 4.2% against 4.9%. The age label differs on
  5.3% (3.8 to 7.4) of the faces and the gender label on 2.5% (1.5 to 4.1). The difference is
  inside the noise of 600 faces. The full 10,954 images were **not** run, so the SPEC 15 item for it stays open.

### Auto-enrollment (SPEC 5A)

The shipped `AutoEnroller` (`min_days` 3, `min_sightings`
5, `max_spread` 0.25,
`join_radius` 0.125, `known_match_threshold`
0.875, `sighting_min_interval_s`
300, `max_clusters` 200, `max_members`
30; days counted from retained members) is fed DINOv2-S embeddings from
`DinoV2Embedder` of whole photos in a simulated multi-day stream: 20 individuals
per stream, 6 days, each individual present on a day with probability
0.8, 1 or 2 sightings that day, each sighting a fresh world entry, 10 streams per
set with different seeds. Each created record is scored by looking up which individual each of its
members came from. **Days are simulated and the photos are not a robot's views**: MPDD dogs are
photographed from several cameras and sequences, Stanford Online Products are catalogue photos of
each item (6 per item here, so an item needs 5 of its 6 photos to land in one cluster).

| Set | Streams | Individuals | Sightings | Records created | Pure (one individual) | Mixing two or more | Individuals with a record | Duplicate records of one individual | Sightings attached to a record | Of those, wrong individual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MPDD dogs | 10 | 200 | 1,321 | 26 | 26 | 0 | 26 | 0 | 49 | 0 |
| SOP coffee maker | 10 | 200 | 1,137 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| SOP kettle | 10 | 200 | 1,137 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| SOP mug | 10 | 200 | 1,137 | 7 | 7 | 0 | 7 | 0 | 8 | 0 |
| SOP stapler | 10 | 200 | 1,137 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| SOP toaster | 10 | 200 | 1,137 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| SOP all | 50 | 1,000 | 5,685 | 9 | 9 | 0 | 9 | 0 | 10 | 0 |

* **No wrong merges in these streams, and few enrollments.** The tight `join_radius` keeps look-alikes
  apart, and the price is fragmentation: one individual photographed differently ends up in many
  candidate clusters (952 clusters left from 200 dogs, 4,857 from
  1,000 products) and rarely reaches 5 sightings in one cluster on 3 days. Only 26 of 200 dogs
  (13%) and 9 of 1,000 products got a record. This is the behaviour
  the SPEC describes ("one animal photographed very differently may split into several clusters");
  whether it is acceptable depends on how repetitive a robot's real views are, which this data
  cannot say.
* The spike's warning (mixed clusters accepted 19 to 24% of the time at its best single spread
  threshold) does not appear here, because the shipped algorithm adds the join radius. No look-alike
  merge was seen in 60 streams, which does not prove none happens; real objects of one
  household (two identical mugs) are the harder case and are not in this data.
* Matching after enrollment (`Matcher` with `kind="entity"` at the identity threshold, or the world
  model's tie-breaking) was not measured.

## Latency, on the Mac (not the CM4) (SPEC 13)

Apple M3 Pro, ONNX Runtime and OpenCV limited to the thread counts shown. The CM4 has four
Cortex-A72 cores with no INT8 dot-product instructions; the SPEC's guessed Mac-to-CM4 factor of
8 to 20x is not measured. The pending measurement is `scripts/bench/run_cm4.py` on the robot (SPEC
15, "To verify on the robot in M0").

### Track start to identity decision

Real time, through `LatestFrameRunner`, `pump` (30 Hz camera stand-in) and `VisionPipeline`, 10 people
enrolled, the person appears in the first frame. The stand-in shows a new LFW photo of the person
every 0.2 s, so each of the 5 burst evaluations (at least `vision.burst_min_gap_s` 0.25 s apart,
3 of 5 votes) sees a different photo. Time is from the first camera frame showing the person to
`person.recognized`, in seconds, p50 / p95 over the trials. Budget: p95 at most 2.5 s on cm4.

| Scenario | Detection | Frame-path / attribute threads | Trials (decisions) | p50 / p95 s | Max s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One new person | 5 Hz | 2 / off | 20 (20) | 1.29 / 1.30 | 1.31 |
| One new person | 8 Hz | 2 / off | 20 (20) | 0.84 / 0.86 | 0.86 |
| Three new people together | 8 Hz | 2 / off | 20 (60) | 0.86 / 1.01 | 1.16 |
| One new person, age and gender job running | 8 Hz | 1 / 1 | 20 (20) | 0.86 / 0.88 | 0.89 |

No track failed to reach a decision within the 6 s window. The times are dominated by the voting
design, not by compute: the third positive vote cannot come before two burst gaps (0.5 s) after
the track is confirmed (2 hits), and the detection rate adds a frame period or two, so the Mac
numbers are close to the floor of about 0.75 s (two detections to confirm the track, then 0.5 s of burst gaps). Compute per frame here is a few
milliseconds (table below); on the CM4 compute may dominate and the 2.5 s budget is open. With the age
and gender job running (INT8, 1 thread, shipped `attribute_threads`), identity was not slowed, and
age and gender were available at 2.0 s (p50) after track start in
17 of 20 tracks (the other 3 stayed `unknown` under the 0.8 rule).
Until then the track counts as a possible minor.

## World map in simulation (SPEC 5A, 14)

MuJoCo Reachy Mini daemon, headless, driven through `ReachyMiniDriver` and `MotionLoop`; the real
`WorldModel`, attention, scan policy and `ReachyScanPlanner` run, as in `test_sim.py`. **Only the
detector is a stand-in**: it reports each planted thing, at its true bearing, whenever the measured
head pose has it inside the camera view (no images, no ONNX, so this tests the map and the
scanning, not recognition). Things are planted at random bearings at least 45 degrees apart and 50
degrees off the home view. A planted person is reported for the first 2 s they are in view and then
"leaves" (otherwise attention on a person holds the robot for the rest of the run). The run ends
when the sweeps have put every planted thing on the map or after 200 s. Idle interval for scans is
set to 10 s to keep runs short. Pass: every planted thing has an entry of its kind within
`world.match_gate_deg` (15 degrees), no extra entry, and all entries fade after the observations stop.
Fading uses virtual time: the world clock is advanced in 5 s steps (the object half-life is 30 minutes;
waiting is not possible).

| Planted | Runs | Pass | Things found within gate | Extra entries | Complete after the first sweep | Wall time to complete, s | Runs where all entries faded |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cat, cup, book | 5 | 5/5 | 15/15 | 0 | 5/5 | 33 to 36 | 5/5 |
| person, cat, cup, book | 5 | 5/5 | 20/20 | 0 | 3/5 | 23 to 56 | 5/5 |

* Every found entry sits exactly at its planted bearing (error 0.0 degrees): the stand-in reports true
  bearings and the base-frame round trip loses nothing, so this checks the map's bookkeeping and
  matching, not a perception error. Head yaw at the end of the runs without a person was within
  0.02 degrees of home; in 3 of the 5 runs with a person the head was still turned toward the person
  (143, 52 and 87 degrees) when the map was complete, because attention held it there.
* In 2 of the 5 runs with a person, the first sweep ended early
  because attention engaged the person (SPEC 5A: never scan while engaged), and a second sweep after
  the idle interval completed the map.
* Fading after removal (seconds until the entry is forgotten; the reference columns are one fresh
  observation in the world model alone, in view and out of view; the measured entries were mostly out of
  view at the end, and gained confidence from repeated sightings during the sweeps, so they last
  longer than the reference; the 3 objects at about 5,980 s were in view and fade at the full rate):

| Kind | In view (reference) s | Out of view (reference) s | Measured after the sweeps, out of view s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| person | 280 | 1375 | 1355 to 1905 (n=5) |
| animal | 700 | 3465 | 4965 to 4980 (n=10) |
| object | 4180 | 20880 | 5980 to 29895 (n=20) |

Out of view, things are remembered for hours by design (`world.out_of_view_decay_factor` 0.2): an
  object seen once is still on the map about 6 hours later (about 8 hours when seen repeatedly). Whether that is what the owner wants is
  an open design question, not a defect of the map.

## Known gaps

* **CM4 not measured.** Every latency here is a Mac number. The CM4 figures for identity, FairFace
  per crop, NanoDet and DINOv2 are still guesses (SPEC 13, 15). Peak RAM on the CM4 is unknown.
* **Pose is not in M1.** The body-pose estimator and its quality measurement belong to the mirror
  milestone (M4); this report has no pose result.
* **Identity** was measured on LFW photos, not on the robot's camera: no real enrollment sessions,
  no children, no low light, no distance. The enrollment quality thresholds (sharpness, pose
  spread) are untuned on real camera data (SPEC 6). The false reject rates are lower bounds.
* **Age and gender:** the full 10,954 FairFace images were not run (1,400 INT8, 600 FP32); crops per
  track are simulated from one photo; FairFace has one photo per face and data overlap with the model
  training is possible; there is no data from real home light. Teen and senior are not distinguishable from adult or from unknown
  with the current grouping and the 0.8 rule (finding above).
* **Objects:** single frames only (the 2-hit confirmation and tracking over time are not measured);
  COCO photos are not a robot's low, wide camera; recall for small objects is 4%.
* **Auto-enrollment:** days and sightings are simulated from catalogue photos; the days a robot
  really spends on a household, repeated views of the same object from a moving head, and identical
  look-alikes are not covered. Matching after enrollment is not measured.
* **World map:** the sim covers the map and scanning with a stand-in detector at exact bearings;
  real detection noise, motion blur during a sweep, and a person walking about are not covered.
  Sound direction on Reachy Mini reports one half-plane, so a sound from behind is mirrored in
  front (SPEC 5A, Scanning): not part of this evaluation.
* Other SPEC 13 budgets (vision trigger to reflex, mimicry, speech paths) are not part of M1 and
  were not measured.

## Reproduce

Needs the datasets and weights in `~/.cache/roboemot-bench/` (`scripts/bench/README.md` fetches
them; `roboemot models list --cache-dir ~/.cache/roboemot-bench/models` should show every model
`ok`). Run one command at a time; scratch files (embedding caches, temporary registries) go to
`$ROBOEMOT_EVAL_SCRATCH` (default under the system temp folder) and are not part of the results.

```bash
python3.13 -m venv .venv && . .venv/bin/activate
pip install -e "packages/roboemot-core[dev,vision]" -e "packages/roboemot-reachymini[dev]" pyarrow
export OMP_NUM_THREADS=2
nice -n 10 python -I scripts/eval/identity.py --k 10 --draws 10 --out identity_k10        # about 4 min
nice -n 10 python -I scripts/eval/identity.py --k 100 --draws 3 --out identity_k100
for part in 0 700; do                                                                    # about 5.5 min each
  nice -n 10 python -I scripts/eval/attributes.py run --precision int8 --start $part --count 700
done
nice -n 10 python -I scripts/eval/attributes.py run --precision fp32 --start 0 --count 350  # about 7 min
nice -n 10 python -I scripts/eval/attributes.py run --precision fp32 --start 350 --count 250
python -I scripts/eval/attributes.py report --out attributes
nice -n 10 python -I scripts/eval/objects.py detect --n 1500
nice -n 10 python -I scripts/eval/objects.py dogs
nice -n 10 python -I scripts/eval/objects.py sop
nice -n 10 python -I scripts/eval/latency.py stages
nice -n 10 python -I scripts/eval/latency.py track --trials 20
```

The world map needs the simulator (`reachy_mini` with MuJoCo; the main checkout's venv has it) and
no `-I`, because the script puts this repository's packages first on `sys.path`:

```bash
scripts/sim-daemon.sh &          # headless MuJoCo daemon on port 8000; stop it afterwards
nice -n 10 python scripts/eval/world_sim.py --set things --runs 5 --out world_sim_things
nice -n 10 python scripts/eval/world_sim.py --set with_person --runs 5 --out world_sim_with_person
kill %1
```

Results written by the scripts carry counts and rates only: no image names, embeddings or home paths
(`common.write_result` refuses them).
