# M3 voice: exit report

Measured on 2026-10-18 (SPEC [section 14](../specs/kickoff.md), Milestones, M3 exit: "Italian and
English conversation within latency budgets"; budgets in [section 13](../specs/kickoff.md), Latency
budgets). Everything here ran on one Mac (Apple M3 Pro, 12 logical cores, 18 GB, macOS 26.6.2,
Python 3.12.13, `onnxruntime` 1.31.0, `vosk` 0.3.44), with the real Silero VAD v5.1.2 and the real
Vosk small Italian and English models. Piper ran as a real worker process in a Linux container (see
"Piper"). **Nothing was measured on the real robot or on a CM4**: the microphone, the speaker, the
room, the LLM and the CM4's CPU are all stand-ins or absent (see "Synthetic").

Reproduce with the commands under "Re-running"; the numbers are from the head of PR 38 on `epic/m3-voice`.

| Barge-in to TTS stop | at most 200 ms | pass: 111 to 167 ms from the first sound (8 runs) | **fail in 5 of 8 runs**: 264 to 300 ms (135, 155 and 166 ms in the others), from the first sound; **open, being fixed** (counted from the first sound, owner decision 2026-10-18) | same |

The English barge-in misses its 200 ms budget (a fricative-initial "stop", see "Barge-in" below); the
test records it as an expected failure instead of passing it. Every figure is a handful of runs on one machine (six with real Piper for the first-audio figures, eight for the barge-in), not a 95th
percentile, and the cm4 column is for a board that did not run any of this.

## What the test does

`packages/roboemot-reachymini/tests/test_m3_exit.py` (`pytest -m models`; needs the `voice` extra and
`roboemot models fetch --voice`). It runs the real `RoboEmotApp` on a scripted SDK client:

* **Microphone**: a paced fake of `media.get_audio_sample()`: 20 ms chunks of 16 kHz mono, each
  returned only once its time has come on the wall clock (real time, not a file read). A recorded
  clip is laid on the stream with 0.5 s of silence before it.
* **Pipeline, all real**: the audio hub (`ReachyAudio`), Silero VAD and endpointing, Vosk streaming
  ASR with partials, the fast path (keywords, wake phrases, hints), the listener, the conversation
  worker, the router and its SSE parsing (over an in-process `httpx.MockTransport`), the sentence
  splitter, the speech output, the speaker with its echo reference, and the barge-in detector with
  its own Silero instance.
* **LLM route**: a mock that waits `FIRST_TOKEN_S` = 0.55 s before the first byte (the middle of a
  LAN server's 400 to 700 ms) and then streams two words every 30 ms. It answers in the language the
  system prompt names ("Always reply in Italian."), so the test checks that the utterance's
  language reaches the prompt and the voice.
* **TTS**: real Piper when `ROBOEMOT_PIPER_PYTHON` points at an interpreter, else a stand-in with a
  fixed first-chunk delay of 0.25 s (about two times what Piper took per sentence below).
* **Speaker**: a recorder behind the real `ReachySpeaker`: it logs the wall time of every
  `push_audio_sample` and of `clear_player`. Nothing plays and nothing comes back to the microphone.

Per language: the wake clip ("ciao robot" / "hello robot") goes in; the test waits for the answer's
first audio; then, while the answer is queued, the stop clip ("fermati" / "stop") goes onto the
microphone and the test waits for `clear_player`. It asserts: the key words in the transcript, the
utterance is addressed and in the right language, one request to the route with the language in
its prompt, every sentence synthesised in that language, the fast-path reaction (and that the greeting keyword was spotted), the first audio
against the cm4 budget on two clocks, the barge-in stop (at most 200 ms from the first sound; the English case is recorded as an
expected failure when it misses), and that nothing is pushed after the stop.

Clocks. "Last speech frame" is `Utterance.last_speech_stamp` (the start of the last 32 ms frame the
VAD judged speech at the off threshold, SPEC 8; the figures `latency_ms.*` of the conversation worker
and `latency.*` of the listener, both in the debug report, count from it). The test also checks first
audio against the microphone clock: the wall time at which the last frame of the clip louder than
-40 dBFS was captured, to the wall time of the first `push_audio_sample`. The two agree within 30 to
110 ms; the second is the larger because it includes the hub's read lag and the VAD's off-threshold
decision.

## Measured

The table is the first three of six runs with real Piper (in a container); the ranges elsewhere use all six (the other three: it 1427, 1371, 1348 ms, en 1383, 1389, 1342 ms on the worker clock). `FIRST_TOKEN_S` = 0.55. All times in ms. "Request" is when
the HTTP request is sent, counted from the last speech frame; the other three columns are
cumulative.

| Language | Run | Request | First token | First sentence | First audio (worker clock) | First audio (mic clock) | Headroom on 2.0 s: extra first-token latency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| it | 1 | 613 | 1264 | 1330 | 1429 | 1515 | +0.57 s |
| it | 2 | 588 | 1202 | 1266 | 1356 | 1454 | +0.64 s |
| it | 3 | 593 | 1200 | 1263 | 1377 | 1486 | +0.62 s |
| en | 1 | 620 | 1192 | 1296 | 1414 | 1473 | +0.59 s |
| en | 2 | 599 | 1160 | 1257 | 1394 | 1465 | +0.61 s |
| en | 3 | 619 | 1180 | 1274 | 1378 | 1406 | +0.62 s |

Where the time goes: about 0.6 s from the last speech frame to the request (`voice.vad.end_silence_ms`
500 ms plus the endpoint frame and the ASR final), 0.55 s of mock first-token latency (the first token
shows at 1.16 to 1.26 s: the request time plus the mock's delay plus the first SSE pieces), 0.06 to 0.1 s to complete the first sentence, and 0.1 s for Piper's first chunk. With
the 0.55 s LAN figure the 2.0 s budget leaves room for a route whose first-token latency is up to about 1.1 to 1.2 s; a
slower server or the 1.5 s route fallback trigger (SPEC 8) would not fit. The npu column (1.5 s)
would still hold at this LLM figure on the worker clock, with 0.07 to 0.15 s to spare, but the npu is not what runs voice here.

With the stand-in TTS (0.25 s first chunk) first audio was 1.49 to 1.56 s in six runs (both languages).

### Fast path

Reaction vs the last speech frame; negative means before the end of speech.

| Case | it | en |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greeting at the start of the sentence ("ciao robot, come stai oggi?"), 3 runs | -1182, -1193, -1421 ms | -1338, -1313, -1294 ms |
| Keyword-final: the utterance is only the keyword ("fermati" / "stop"), 3 runs | -800, -793, -805 ms | -766, -741, -730 ms |

The first row "fires before end of speech": the keyword fires from the partial hypothesis as soon as
the word is recognised, early in the sentence, so the 300 ms assertion cannot fail there. The second
row is the non-vacuous check (`test_a_keyword_final_utterance_reacts_inside_300_ms_of_the_last_speech_frame`):
the keyword is the last word, and the reaction still comes 0.73 to 0.81 s before the last speech frame
because the partial shows it before the speech ends. The final transcript comes 0.55 to 0.78 s after
the last speech frame (core test `test_voice_asr_models.py`: the end silence plus the ASR finish); a
reaction that needed the final transcript would be over 300 ms, so this budget holds only because
the fast path runs on partials.

### Barge-in

Stop = the time from the first audible sound of the user's "stop" word (the first 32 ms frame of the
clip louder than -40 dBFS, the "true speech start" of the core barge-in test) to the speaker's
`clear_player`. Eight runs per language with real Piper:

| Language | Stop after the first sound | Result against 200 ms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| it ("fermati") | 137, 111, 121, 125, 167, 128, 121, 137 ms | pass in 8 of 8 |
| en ("stop") | 300, 135, 278, 268, 166, 280, 264, 155 ms | **fail in 5 of 8** |

The English test case ends in `pytest.xfail` when it misses (not strict, because some runs pass); the
other assertions of the case still apply. So the English barge-in budget is **not met** by this
exit test. The reason is the word: the English clip starts with a 130 ms /s/ at -40 to -29 dBFS that
Silero scores at only 0.35 to 0.67, and the voiced part starts at about 160 ms. The stop then needs
`voice.barge_in.min_speech_ms` (96 ms) of frames at or above `voice.barge_in.threshold` (0.8). Measured
from where a fresh Silero first scores 0.8 (the "voiced onset", printed by the test as a diagnostic
only: it read 140, -25, 118, 108 and 6 ms, a noisy proxy because the test's Silero sees frames
aligned to the clip and the app's sees them aligned to the 20 ms chunk boundaries) the English stop
would be within 200 ms, but that is not the budget: the owner decided (2026-10-18) that the SPEC 13
line "barge-in to TTS stop" counts from the first sound, for every language. So this is a real
defect: open, being fixed in a follow-up slice. Shortening `voice.barge_in.min_speech_ms` (minimum 32 ms, default 96) would take about 64 ms
off, at the price of more false triggers; that is a tuning decision for the robot.

The cut itself is fast: the stop reached `clear_player` and no audio was pushed afterwards in every
run, and 0.6 to 2.0 s of queued answer was dropped.

### Child case (Italian)

A minor is present (the app's minor signal forced on), no route is cleared for minors and
`safety.minors_allowed_llm` is off (its default): the greeting is answered with the built-in
`safety.minor_lines.it.greeting`, **no request is sent to the route**, and the first audio is at
0.88 to 0.99 s (with the 0.25 s stand-in TTS), with the master switch off and also with
`safety.minors_allowed_llm` on but the route not cleared (`allowed_for_minors` false). The English
child line is not run.

## Wake phrases on synthetic voices

The exit clips use the designed wake phrases. They were chosen after a survey, because the first
clips ("ciao Reachy", "hello Reachy") did not wake the robot. 36 clips, macOS `say` voices, through
the same VAD, Vosk and wake logic (`addressed` for a stranger, `voice.wake_word_policy`
`unless_enrolled_in_view`):

| Phrase | Result |
| --- | --- |
| "Ciao robot" (it, 5 voices) | woke with 4 of 5 (Alice: heard "ciao roby") |
| "Ehi robot" (it, 5 voices) | woke with **0** of 5 (heard "arabo", "è il robot", "i robot") |
| "Hey robot" / "Hello robot" (en, 7 voices) | woke with 4 of 7 each: Samantha, Daniel, Fred and Ralph; Eddy, Flo and Kathy heard "robots", "robert" |
| "Ciao Reachy" (it) and "Hello Reachy" (en), name alias path | 0 of 12 as a wake: the name came out as "reati" (Alice only), "laureati", "reais", "riyadh she", "reactionary", "reality"; the alias count as a wake only in at most `voice.fastpath.alias_wake_max_words` (4) words, and a full sentence is longer |

The original Italian clip of the core tests (Alice, "ciao Reachy, come stai?") woke the robot only
because Vosk wrote "ciao reati come stai": four words, the alias limit. The original English clip
("hello Reachy, how are you today?") did not: "hello riyadh she how are you today" is seven words. The numbers are for synthetic voices that the models were not tuned on; real speakers will differ,
in either direction. The wake phrase is an interim decision (SPEC 8 stage 2) and is an open item
for the robot, below. The robot's name is not fixed to "Reachy" any more: it is `voice.wake_word`
or the name the owner gave the robot in the daemon setup, and its `name_wake_templates` phrases
("ciao Neve") only apply when the name is in the recognizer vocabulary ("neve" is, in both small
models; "reachy" is not). A synthetic "Neve" was recognised about one time in four, so the generic
phrases stay on. The bare name emits `speech.name_called` wherever it occurs in a text but wakes
the robot only in a final of at most `voice.fastpath.alias_wake_max_words` words, so "è arrivata la
neve oggi pomeriggio" does not wake a robot called Neve. Not measured again on the robot.

## Piper

Real Piper does not run on macOS: the `piper-tts` macOS wheels ship without a usable espeak data
path (upstream). The test therefore ran the real `piper_worker.py` (unchanged, the protocol of
`roboemot.voice.piper_backend`) inside a Linux/aarch64 container, with the voice files read from
the Mac's voice models folder through a bind mount:

```sh
# image: python:3.12-slim + piper-tts==1.8.0 (the pin of the installer)
printf 'FROM python:3.12-slim\nRUN pip install --no-cache-dir piper-tts==1.8.0\n' | docker build -t roboemot-piper-test -
# an "interpreter" wrapper: the backend runs `<python> -I piper_worker.py --voice ... --chunk-ms ...`
printf '#!/bin/sh\nexec docker run -i --rm -v /Users:/Users roboemot-piper-test python "$@"\n' > piper-docker.sh
chmod +x piper-docker.sh
ROBOEMOT_PIPER_PYTHON=$PWD/piper-docker.sh pytest -m models packages/roboemot-reachymini/tests/test_m3_exit.py -s
```

(The path of the bind mount must be the same inside the container, because the backend passes
absolute paths; `voice.models_dir` of the test is the user's voice models folder, override with
`ROBOEMOT_VOICE_MODELS_DIR`.) The two voices are `it_IT-paola-medium` and `en_US-amy-medium`, both
22.05 kHz. The container adds about a second of start-up (the test waits for the warm-up of the
conversation language before it starts the clip; `voice.piper.startup_timeout_s` is 60 s) and its
virtualised CPU is not a CM4's. The worker's first chunk took 65 to 111 ms per sentence (core test
`test_tts_models.py`, same container). Without the variable the test uses the stand-in TTS and
still runs everything else. During this work Docker Desktop once stopped starting containers
after several were killed mid-start; `docker desktop restart` fixed it.

Without Docker, `ROBOEMOT_PIPER_PYTHON` can point at any Linux interpreter that has `piper-tts`,
including the one the installer makes on the robot (`roboemot.paths.piper_python`).

## Synthetic, and what that hides

* **Voices**: the four original clips and the two wake clips are macOS `say` output (Alice, Samantha,
  and Flo for "ciao robot"), 16 kHz mono, clean, no noise, no room, no accent, no hesitation. Vosk's
  error rate on real children, accents or a TV in the room is not measured here.
* **No echo**: the fake speaker does not feed the microphone. The barge-in's echo guard (the
  cross-correlation against the playback reference) is exercised by the core tests with synthetic
  and smeared echo, and by `test_the_robots_own_speech_in_the_microphone_does_not_stop_it_on_real_models`,
  but not through the real XMOS AEC, speaker, cabinet and room.
* **Mocked LLM**: `FIRST_TOKEN_S` = 0.55 s and a 30 ms token gap are a plausible LAN model, not a
  measurement. No cloud route, no tools, no secret store (cloud keys are still empty).
* **Fake SDK media**: 20 ms mic chunks, 16 kHz in and out, `clear_player` that returns at once. The
  real chunk size, the capture and playback lag, the SDK's own buffering and the time
  `clear_player` takes are unknown, and every one adds to the figures above.
* **Mac, not CM4**: Silero (two instances), Vosk, the FFT of the echo guard and Piper all share one
  CM4 with vision, the daemon and the motion loop. These runs had 12 cores to themselves.
* **Stand-in TTS** when Piper is not given (0.25 s first chunk, a quiet tone about 0.3 s per
  word): the numbers for it are marked in the test's output.
* **Counts**: three runs per cell with Piper, one machine. No percentile.

## Left for the robot and the owner

Facts to measure on Neve (they are in SPEC 15 and `TODO.md`, once each):

* Residual echo through the Wireless's XMOS AEC loopback, and whether `voice.echo_tail_ms` (300 ms)
  and the speaking gate are enough with the real speaker; whether the media layer cancels the
  robot's own voice at all on other boards (SPEC 15, M0 list).
* Barge-in tuning with real playback: `voice.barge_in.threshold` (0.8), `min_speech_ms` (96),
  `echo_corr_max` (0.6), `echo_max_lag_ms` (300), `pre_roll_ms` (64); the English "stop" start
  above; a user quieter than the echo is not heard by design.
* Capture and playback lag against `voice.barge_in.echo_max_lag_ms`, and the real latency of
  `clear_player`, which the audio-clock figures leave out.
* CM4 load: Silero twice, Vosk, the FFT per speech frame and Piper together, with vision and the
  daemon running; Vosk's RAM (both small models: about 250 MB is the plan's estimate, not measured; 155 MB on disk)
  against the 2.4 GB budget.
* Real chunk size and rate of `get_audio_sample()` (the hub reads them, the test assumes 20 ms).
* The Piper virtualenv on the robot: `python -m venv` needs `ensurepip` there (the installer
  creates it with the robot's Python), and the install is recorded for uninstall. Only the dry run
  has run on Neve.
* The wake phrases ("ehi robot", "ciao robot", "hey robot", "hello robot") and the aliases
  ("reati", "riyadh she") are an interim choice that failed on several synthetic voices (table
  above). They stay as they are (owner, 2026-10-18): synthetic-voice recall says little about
  natural voices. Confirm with real speakers.
* Italian quality of the OpenAI speech voices (the cloud TTS backend), unlistened to.

Decisions for the owner:

* Piper voice licences are still unverified: each MODEL_CARD says "License: See URL" and points at a dataset
  (`voice/models.yaml`).
* `piper_worker.py` imports the GPL-3.0 `piper` package at run time and runs in its own process and
  environment. Decided (owner, 2026-10-18): a GPL subprocess is acceptable. Resolved.
* To talk to a child both `safety.minors_allowed_llm` and a route's `allowed_for_minors` must be
  on; the default minor blocklist and the built-in child lines (`safety.minor_lines`) keep their
  defaults until the web UI can edit them (owner, 2026-10-18).
* The secret store for `${secret:...}` route keys does not exist, so cloud routes send an empty key;
  `privacy.pseudonyms_to_cloud` is refused by the config validator until it is implemented.
* Decided (2026-10-18): "barge-in to TTS stop, at most 200 ms" counts from the first sound, for every
  language. The English miss is open, being fixed.

## Re-running

```sh
pip install -e "packages/roboemot-core[dev,voice]" -e "packages/roboemot-reachymini[dev]"
roboemot models fetch --voice
pytest -m models packages/roboemot-reachymini/tests/test_m3_exit.py -s        # stand-in TTS
ROBOEMOT_PIPER_PYTHON=/path/to/piper/venv/bin/python pytest -m models packages/roboemot-reachymini/tests/test_m3_exit.py -s
ROBOEMOT_EXIT_REPORT=/tmp/m3.json pytest -m models ...                         # also write the numbers as JSON
```

CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) has no job that runs `-m models` tests and no model cache, so this
test is not run there; adding it needs a cached voice models folder (about 0.3 GB: Silero, two
Vosk models, two Piper voices) and a Piper interpreter on the runner.
