# Spike: M1 vision models (accuracy, licence, CPU speed)

Status: measurement report; decisions below. Section references
are to `docs/specs/kickoff.md` (SPEC 5, 5A, 6, 13, 15). Scripts and raw numbers:
`scripts/bench/` (README has the repro commands) and `scripts/bench/results/*.json`.

## Decision (2026-10-16, user)

1. Face stack: permissive, YuNet (detect) plus SFace (embed). `identity.match_threshold` stays 0.45 (the SFace seed).
2. Age and gender on cm4: the FairFace ViT-B pair (Apache-2.0), run once per new track at low priority; age is unknown (possible minor) until it returns. InsightFace `genderage` is rejected (it labels 86% of minors as adult).
3. INT8 for age and gender on cm4 (2026-10-16, user, after the INT8 FairFace section below): the cm4 profile loads the dynamically quantized (QInt8) FairFace pair, `vision.attribute_precision: auto` (INT8 for the cm4 manifest profile, FP32 otherwise), built by `roboemot models fetch` from the verified FP32 files (`models prune --sources` frees the FP32 afterwards). Crops per track are configurable, `vision.attribute_crops` default 3 (1 to 10, not below `identity.estimate_frames_min`, now default 3), to be tuned after `run_cm4.py` on Neve; the 0.8 rule applies at any count.
4. Auto-enrollment for animals and objects with DINOv2-S: `max_spread` 0.25, `min_days` 3, at least 5 sightings (judgement, to tune on the robot). Objects separate poorly (66% rank-1), so wrong merges are expected and the owner fixes them in the web UI.

The SPEC was updated to match (sections 5, 5A, 6, 13, 15; item 3 sections 5 and 13). The analysis below is unchanged.

## Summary

- On LFW all candidates are within about one point of each other (99.3 to 99.6% with the
  centre face selected), so LFW does not separate them. The heavy ArcFace R50 buys little on LFW.
  Harder sets (cross-pose, low resolution, children) were not run.
- Permissive face stack (YuNet MIT + SFace Apache-2.0) vs best non-commercial stack
  (SCRFD + w600k): 0.2 points lower accuracy when the centre face is selected, 0.4 to 0.6
  points lower with the largest-face rule. Latency on the Mac is similar to the non-commercial
  MobileFaceNet stack.
- The InsightFace `genderage.onnx` is not usable for the minor-present policy: on FairFace it
  labels 86% of minors as adult or senior. The only FairFace-trained permissive model found
  (ViT-B, Apache-2.0) is far better but 343 MB and about 200 ms per call on the Mac.
- Individual animals: DINOv2-S (Apache-2.0) is the best and is permissive; rank-1 97.5% on 191 dogs.
  Individual household objects from a product catalogue are much worse (rank-1 about 66 to 69%);
  that set is pessimistic for a robot (see limits) but does not support auto-enrolling objects.
- Nothing was measured on the CM4. The CM4 column is a labelled guess.

## Age group and gender (FairFace validation, padding 0.25, n = 10,954)

SPEC groups from FairFace bins: child = 0-2 and 3-9, teen = 10-19, adult = 20-59, senior = 60+.
The 10-19 bin cannot be split at 13, so it is scored as teen; "minor" is bins 0-19 (n = 2,736).
Error rates use only predictions that pass the 0.8 confidence rule (SPEC 5); "unknown" is the
fraction withheld. Single-image estimates; the SPEC aggregates at least 5 frames, so these are
pessimistic for gender and age error but give a floor on per-frame behaviour.

- genderage.onnx: SCRFD-500M box, then InsightFace attribute crop (96x96). Gender confidence
  is the softmax of the two gender logits. The age head is a regression and has no confidence,
  so the 0.8 rule does not apply to age (unknown = no face found only: 0.2%). Input
  normalisation checked on 498 samples: raw 0 to 255 RGB gives 78.5% gender accuracy
  (argmax), `(x-127.5)/128` gives 48.2%, so the former is used.
- FairFace ViT-B: whole 224x224 image, softmax. Age confidence is the summed probability of the
  bins in the SPEC group. Possible train/validation overlap cannot be excluded: the model card
  evaluates on its own 10,000-image split and does not say it held out FairFace's validation set.

Reading it:

- "Minors labelled adult/senior" counts only confident adult/senior predictions; an `unknown`
  is not an error (SPEC 5: unknown counts as a possible minor). For the ViT, 11.3% of minors
  still pass as confident adult or senior; without the 0.8 rule it is 19.8% (argmax over the
  nine bins, total age-group error 13.0%). Adults mislabelled as child or teen: 0.8% (genderage),
  0.0% (ViT). Both models err in the less conservative direction for minors.
- genderage has no usable confidence for age, so the "unknown covers uncertainty" mechanism of SPEC 5
  does nothing for it; it would label 86% of minors as adult. Gender errors are 15 to 23% depending on race group
  (Middle Eastern, White, Latino lower; Black, East and Southeast Asian, Indian higher).
- The ViT's gender error is higher for female faces (6.4%) than male (2.5%) and for Black
  faces (7.9%); the 0.8 rule withholds 5.1% of genders and 25.5% of ages.
- ViT-B at 343 MB and about 200 ms (Mac, 2 threads) per call, two models: not realistic on the CM4
  at "every 10 s" with the 2-core budget (guess 1.6 to 4 s per call). A distilled or smaller
  FairFace-trained model would be needed; none with verifiable permissive weights was found
  (searched the Hugging Face hub for FairFace age and gender models; the others found had
  `license:other`, no licence, or unknown provenance).

## Individual animals and objects (SPEC 5A, 15 spike)

Embeddings: whole image (or crop) squashed to the model input, L2-normalised (DINOv2-S CLS token;
CLIP and MobileCLIP image-embedding output).

- Dogs: MPDD (CC BY 4.0), n = 1,657 images of 191 identities (the page says 192; one id is
  missing in the archive), whole-body photos with pose variation, phone camera.
- Objects: Stanford Online Products test split, seeded subset of 5 household categories
  (mug, kettle, stapler, toaster, coffee maker): 750 items, 4,004 images (150 items per
  category with at least 4 images, at most 6 images per item). Product-listing photos from many
  viewpoints and backgrounds, which is harder than a fixed robot camera on a known mug.
- Rank-1: leave-one-out, each image queries all others. Same-category rank-1 restricts the gallery
  to the query's category (hard negatives: a mug among mugs).
- No dataset here records capture days, so the SPEC's `min_days` cannot be tested.
  MPDD file names carry a `cXsY` tag (I assume camera and sequence; not documented in the files I
  read): rank-1 excluding positives with the same tag is within 0.3 points of the plain one
  (`rank1_cross_session` in the JSON), so that tag does not drive the result.
- Pairwise separation: d' and EER over all same vs different pairs. Thresholds at FAR of 1e-2
  and 1e-3 over all different-individual pairs (1.36M for dogs, 8.0M for objects; 1.59M same-category).
  Raw JSON: `results/individuals.json`.

Observations:

- DINOv2-S is both the best on dogs and permissive, so there is no accuracy/licence tradeoff for
  animal embeddings. The CLIP family has a compressed cosine range (different dogs average 0.75
  for CLIP B/32), so a fixed threshold must be model-specific.
- At FAR 1e-3 DINOv2-S on dogs accepts only 61% of true pairs (threshold 0.71): a single
  frame match is unreliable; identity needs voting over several frames, as SPEC 6 already does for faces.
- Objects are much worse (rank-1 about 66 to 69% for all three, EER 15% any-negatives, 23 to 26%
  against same-category negatives). Since the SOP views vary a lot per item this likely
  understates a fixed camera, but there is no measurement here that supports auto-enrolling
  objects. MobileCLIP-S0 is not better than DINOv2-S despite the research-only licence.

### Auto-enrollment cluster spread (SPEC 5A `world.autoenroll.max_spread`)

Spread of a candidate cluster = max over members of (1 - cosine to the cluster centroid).
"Pure" clusters are k random images of one individual; "mixed" clusters take about half the images
from a second individual of the same category (the failure to avoid: two cats merged). 3,000
clusters of each kind per k, seeded. "Best" maximises P(accept pure) minus P(accept mixed) for
a single `max_spread`.

For dogs a single `max_spread` separates pure from mixed clusters with about 4 to 7% of
mixed clusters wrongly accepted at DINOv2 (best of the three models). For objects the pure and

threshold both enrols real objects and rejects look-alikes in this data.

## INT8 FairFace

Question: can INT8 quantization keep the FairFace ViT-B age and gender accuracy while cutting
time and RAM enough for the CM4 (SPEC 5, 13)?

Method (repro in `scripts/bench/README.md`): ONNX Runtime 1.31 `quantize_dynamic`, weight type
QInt8, defaults otherwise (`scripts/bench/quantize.py`). Dynamic means weights are INT8 and
activations are quantized per call; no calibration data. Result: 343.4 MB to 86.9 MB per model
(about 3.95x smaller). I did not try QUInt8 because QInt8 worked and matched FP32. Static
quantization (calibrated activations) and quantizing the attention softmax/LayerNorm path were
not tried. Measured on a Mac M3 Pro, shared machine, `nice -n 10`, ORT intra_op = 2 and
inter_op = 1 for the accuracy runs (the ViT graph is sequential, so inter_op has no effect), intra_op
1 or 2 and inter_op = 2 for the speed runs, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=2`.

### CM4 (estimate only, not measured)

No CM4 numbers exist for this section. Using the same assumption as the speed table above
(CM4 is 8 to 20x slower than the Mac at 2 threads), the INT8 pair would take about 1.1 to 2.8 s
per crop (FP32: 3.0 to 7.5 s). Two reasons this could be off in either direction: the 8 to 20x
factor was measured on no CM4 at all, and the Cortex-A72 has no INT8 dot-product instructions
(those arrived in Armv8.2), so INT8 may gain less over FP32 on the CM4 than the 2.7 to 3.0x seen
on the M3. Peak RSS of 289 MB for the pair (about 225 MB over the baseline) is a measured Mac
figure; ORT on aarch64 Linux may differ by tens of MB. Run `run_cm4.py` on the robot to replace
this paragraph with real numbers.

### Recommendation

- Accuracy: INT8 dynamic QInt8 is, within the noise of n = 1,400, as accurate as FP32 (age error
  +0.2 points, gender error -0.2 points, 97% / 99% per-sample agreement). Minors labelled
  adult/senior are 0.8 points higher (3 of 357 minors); not significant, but it is the one metric
  where the direction is unfavourable.
- Adopt INT8 for the CM4 profile: it is the only variant with a plausible path to the CM4 budget
  (86.9 MB per model, 289 MB RSS for the pair), and FP32 is not (760 MB RSS, an estimated 3 to
  7 s per crop). Before relying on it, (1) run `run_cm4.py` on the robot (the INT8 speed-up on an
  A72 is unverified), and (2) re-run the accuracy on the full 10,954 images (about 30 minutes for
  INT8 alone) if the minor-safety direction matters for the decision.
- Crops per track: the aggregation is over crops of one track, run once per new track at low
  priority. At the estimated 1.1 to 2.8 s per crop, 5 crops cost 6 to 14 s of the vision cores,
  3 crops 3 to 8 s, 1 crop 1 to 3 s. Recommend 3 crops on the CM4 if the measured pair time is
  under about 2 s, and 1 crop (age stays `unknown` until a second crop agrees, which keeps the
  SPEC 5 minor-safe default) if it is above; 5 crops only on the npu profile. These counts are my
  reading of the estimate, not a measurement. Under the 0.8 rule roughly a quarter of single
  crops give an unknown age already, so a single crop leaves many tracks `unknown` (which is the
  safe direction).
- No SPEC change is proposed here; adopting INT8 and the crop count is the owner's decision.

### Proposed defaults (per model, to be confirmed on the robot)

`identity.match_threshold` (cosine): the measured FAR 1e-4 threshold over 29.6M LFW
different-identity pairs, rounded up. LFW is frontal celebrity photos; a robot camera adds
pose, blur and lighting, which raise impostor similarity, so these are floors. SPEC 6
already adds the 3-of-5 vote and a 0.1 margin on top.

| Model | FAR 1e-3 | FAR 1e-4 | Proposed seed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| w600k_mbf | 0.228 | 0.283 | 0.30 |
| w600k_r50 | 0.203 | 0.258 | 0.28 |
| SFace | 0.362 to 0.368 | 0.419 to 0.424 | 0.45 |

The SPEC's global default of 0.45 sits far above the ArcFace-family values (it matches SFace).
Using 0.45 with mbf would be very conservative (many missed matches); the spike cannot say what
the right trade-off is without on-robot images. Keep the SPEC "tuned per model" wording and
store the seed with the model id.

`world.autoenroll` (animals and objects, SPEC 5A):

- Limit auto-enrollment to animals. Objects: do not auto-enrol by appearance alone on this
  evidence (mixed clusters are accepted 19 to 24% of the time at the best threshold, while
  32 to 47% of genuine clusters are rejected). Offer them to the owner as suggestions.
- Embedding: DINOv2-S. `max_spread` is defined above as max (1 - cosine to centroid); with 3 to 5
  sightings in a cluster, 0.23 (k=3) to 0.26 (k=5) accepted 94 and 88% of pure dog clusters and
  4 and 7% of mixed ones. A conservative start of 0.20 would reject more look-alikes at the cost
  of slower enrolment; the data above does not show which the owner prefers. The value must be
  re-derived if the embedder changes (CLIP-style models need about 0.07 to 0.09 on the same scale).
- `min_days`: not measured (no dataset records days). I would keep it at 3 and require at least 5
  sightings in total, but that is a judgement, not a result. It is the main protection against
  merging two similar animals seen in one session, which these numbers cannot show.
- Cost: 76 ms per embedding on the Mac (0.6 to 1.5 s guessed on the CM4), so compute it once per
  track and then at most every few seconds, with the governor lowering the rate first.

### Method

- Data: COCO val2017 (5,000 images), a seeded random subset of 1,500 images (`numpy` PCG64 seed 0,
  `scripts/bench/eval_objects.py --n 1500 --seed 0`), the same images for both models. Images are
  fetched one at a time from the COCO host (it serves HTTP; its HTTPS certificate does not match the
  host name); the annotations zip was checked against the S3 ETag (md5 `f4bbac64...e5fa2c`).
- Classes: person, cat, dog, bird, cup, bottle, chair, couch, potted plant, tv, laptop, book, remote,
  cell phone, bowl, teddy bear. Detections of other classes are dropped before scoring. Person is
  scored but is not shipped (faces come from YuNet), so means below are over the other 15 classes.
- AP50: pycocotools 2.0.11, IoU 0.5, 101-point interpolation, maxDets 100, all object sizes
  (including the many tiny instances; this is a harsh test for a 320 px input). Score floor 0.01,
  class-wise NMS at each model's own evaluation value (YOLOX 0.65, NanoDet 0.6).
- Recall and precision: greedy matching by score at IoU 0.5, class-aware, crowd regions ignored, at a
  fixed score threshold, same detections as AP.
- Speed: network only, random input, `run_cm4.py` (100 runs, 1 process per model), ONNX Runtime 1.31.0,
  `intra_op_num_threads` 1 or 2 and `inter_op_num_threads=1` (as shipped), Python 3.12.13 (the Setup table above says 3.13 for the earlier runs), `nice -n 10`, Apple M3 Pro. Full `detect()` of the shipped backend
  (resize, normalise, run, decode, NMS) on a 640x480 COCO image: 200 runs.
- Instance counts per class in the subset are small for several classes (below), so single-class
  differences of a few points are within noise; the means are the safer comparison. No confidence
  intervals were computed.

### Recommendation

Accuracy per millisecond favours NanoDet-Plus-m 320: higher mean AP50 (41.0 vs 38.6), more recall at
the same precision at 0.4, and 22% less network time at 2 threads (9.7 vs 12.4 ms) from a smaller
input. The AP gap of 2.4 points is within what 44 to 513 instances per class can hide, so it is a
tie on accuracy and a win on speed and on upstream's own mAP (27.0 vs 25.8). Both are Apache-2.0
with official ONNX. NanoDet-Plus-m 320 ships. Decision rule from the task: Mac p50 at 2 threads
of the full detector is 11.3 ms (at most about 15 ms), so `vision.objects_enabled` defaults to on,
at the SPEC 13 rate of 1 fps (`vision.rates.objects_hz`).

Caveats that this spike did not close:

- The detector is not the expensive part of the object path: each new animal or object track also
  gets DINOv2-S appearance embeddings (76 ms on the Mac, guessed 0.6 to 1.5 s on the CM4), up to
  `vision.max_embeds_per_frame` (2) per frame during the new-track burst. With several objects in
  view this can dominate; the governor lowers rates under load, but the total on the CM4 is not
  measured. If Neve shows pressure, lower `vision.rates.objects_hz` or `max_embeds_per_frame`, or turn
  `vision.objects_enabled` off.
- Scores below 0.4 are noisy (precision 41% at 0.3). The tracker's own `high_score` (0.6) would have
  hidden everything between 0.4 and 0.6, so animals and objects now use `vision.object_score_min` as
  the start-of-track score (with no low-score pass: nothing below it reaches the tracker) and live for
  the larger of `vision.object_lost_s` (5 s) and `vision.object_lost_periods` (3) object-detection
  periods at the rate the governor allows now, instead of the face value (1.5 s), which would drop a
  track after one missed frame at 1 fps or at any slower rate.
- Category names shown to the world model, placeholders and scene text are readable (`potted plant`,
  via `vision.object_display_names`, else underscores become spaces); English only.
- The app degrades when the NanoDet file is missing or unverified: vision keeps running, objects are
  off and the debug report says why (`roboemot.vision` -> `objects`).
- Birds, cups, bottles, books and remotes are found 5 to 22% of the time at 0.4 on COCO-sized
  objects; do not rely on these for anything but a hint to the owner.
